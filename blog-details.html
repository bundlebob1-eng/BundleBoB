Job costing

Why your job-cost report is three weeks stale

By the time the job-cost report reaches the people who could act on it, the numbers are old enough that the owner meeting runs on a figure nobody fully trusts. Here is where the lag comes from.

Three systems, three schedules

Committed cost lives in the project management system. Actuals land in accounting on their own posting schedule. The change orders that move the number sit in a PM's inbox or a spreadsheet until someone keys them in. Each source is reasonable on its own; together they never agree at the same moment.

Where the days go

Posting lag Vendor invoices and payroll hit accounting days after the work.
Change-order drift Approved COs not yet reflected in committed cost or the owner's system.
Manual assembly Someone exports, pastes and reconciles by hand, once a month.

What closes the gap

Reconcile the systems on a nightly schedule instead of at month-end, surface the specific jobs where they disagree, and fix the data-entry gaps behind the disagreements. The report stops being a monthly archaeology exercise and becomes something a PM can read on a Tuesday.
