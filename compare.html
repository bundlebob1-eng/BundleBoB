Why one embedded engineer, and not the alternatives

There are four honest ways to close the gap between your systems: add one embedded engineer, hire your own integration developer, buy a SaaS integration tool, or bring in a consultancy. We are not the right answer for everyone, and this page says where we are not.

We are probably a fit if…

  • •You run projects or jobs, and your accounting system and your operations system do not talk to each other.
  • •You find out whether a job made money after it is finished, not while you can still act on it.
  • •Someone on your team re-enters the same record into two systems every week.
  • •You have tried to hire for exactly this and could not find the right person.

Don’t hire us if…

  • •You already have two or more developers who know your systems and will still be there next year.
  • •You want a fully autonomous system that acts without a person in the loop.

We work with any business that runs work as jobs — general and specialty contractors, fabrication and machine shops, auto and equipment service, field service and installation. If your work carries costs against a job and the reporting arrives too late to use, the problem is the same one.

For the person who owns the ERP

You have heard this before and it broke.

So here is what is different, stated before you ask.

  • •We start read-only. Write access is something you grant later, if you ever want to.
  • •The mapping is yours. A table in plain language you can read, keep and hand to anyone — not configuration buried inside our product.
  • •When the two systems disagree, we say so. Both values, which rule each side applied, and which record. Nothing fails quietly.
  • •Every screen shows how fresh it is. A dashboard that admits it is stale is worth more than one that quietly is.
Security & data

Plainly: what is in place, and what is only design intent

The engineer will be inside your accounting and subcontractor data. You deserve a straight account of where the controls actually stand today — this is a pre-pilot demo, so most of it is design intent, not implemented and audited fact.

In place now

  • •A stated design principle that every client’s data is isolated per tenant, and that no client data trains anything shared.
  • •Advisory-not-autonomous as a hard product rule: a person approves every write-back to your systems.
  • •A commitment to source-traceability — every figure links to the record it came from.

Design intent — not yet in place

  • •Third-party audit or certification (e.g. SOC 2). None held. None in progress that we would claim.
  • •SSO / SAML, SCIM provisioning, role-based access, field-level audit logging — intended, not built.
  • •Independent penetration testing and a published data-flow diagram — planned before any pilot.

When a control moves from intent to implemented, it moves columns here — not before.
