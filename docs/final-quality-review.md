# Final media, motion and conversion review

## Scope

The owner requested a final desktop, phone and iPad quality pass, a strict three-color brand palette with purple removed, and a review against the latest Briq site. The owner supplied **contact@bundlebob.com** as the public enquiry destination.

## Competitive review

[Briq](https://briq.ai/) was inspected in a real browser at desktop and phone sizes. Its strongest patterns are a direct opening proposition, clear product grouping, a sustained problem-to-solution narrative, repeated conversion links and an explicit implementation story. Its dense uppercase typography and construction-only proposition are not the right fit for BundleBoB’s broader audience.

BundleBoB’s response is clearer service choices, earlier access to its genuine mechanical/HVAC story, a concrete supplier-invoice workflow, human-led delivery, and a reachable email destination. This is a design and usability comparison, not evidence that one business performs better than another. No competitor metrics or product claims were adopted.

## Defects corrected

- The old phone hero was a 1280×720 landscape export at approximately 0.65 Mbps, enlarged into a tall viewport. Its crop discarded much of the frame and magnified compression. New variants are rendered directly from licensed source files, with individual subject framing.
- Desktop hero: 2560×1440; portrait phone: 1080×1920; portrait iPad: 1440×1920. Lighter versions retain those crops on connections reporting both low bandwidth and high latency; a low bandwidth estimate alone does not downgrade a fast connection. The same treatments cover the construction hero. Landscape tablets use the desktop edit. The main loop is now 16 seconds; the hardware shot is removed to keep attention on people and operations.
- The two lower photographic zoom loops are replaced by actual licensed footage: administrative work (10 seconds) and collaboration (8 seconds). Their poster frames come from the films.
- Native H.264 exports put their index before media data for progressive playback. Only the selected source loads, only while visible. Responsive HTML posters work before JavaScript; supporting-film posters load near their sections. Rotation selects the appropriate new crop and preserves the playback position. Hidden tabs, reduced motion and data-saving connections pause/defer media.
- The workflow formerly transformed entire cards, rasterizing their text. Foreground content now stays untransformed. Only decorative paper surfaces carry perspective. Card heights follow content, and short screens/tablets use normal flow instead of a pinned scene.
- Purple and yellow accents are removed. Brand colors are ink `#1B1C1E`, ivory `#F7F7F3` and orange `#FF5A00`, with neutral/transparent surface treatments. A rendered-color audit checks every public page for other saturated interface hues. Photography retains its natural colors.
- The contact page and footer expose the supplied address. The form prepares a correctly addressed email; the visitor reviews and sends it in their own mail application. The site does not falsely claim a server submission succeeded.

## Publication checks

Full review: 160 page/layout combinations and 96 links, with no browser/layout errors. Local mobile Lighthouse: 96 performance; 100 accessibility, best practices and SEO. The throttled phone loop recorded no dropped frames. See `docs/verification.md` and `audit/final-patch/` for results, test conditions and screenshots. Coverage includes phone, portrait/landscape iPad and desktop, Chrome and WebKit playback, rotation, reduced motion, scrolling surfaces, readable text, service selectors, all public routes and the email draft.

Browser emulation is not a physical-device test. The mailbox address is owner-confirmed; no test email was sent and delivery was not asserted. Automated accessibility scans do not establish full conformance. Higher-quality media files are larger; reduced-motion and data-saving visitors retain appropriately cropped posters.
