# Across industries, with a direct engineering partner

BundleBoB is positioned as a cross-industry technology services business. Construction remains a worked example and a specialty page; it does not define the whole business. Industry examples are possible applications, not evidence of completed projects or certifications.

## Typography

Locally hosted Montserrat is the display face; Inter is the body and interface face. The revision increases small mobile labels to 10px, uses 15px for service descriptions, and reduces over-tight heading tracking. Browser checks verify both font families load. No prompt deliverable was requested: the owner clarified that “prompt” meant “font.”

## Scroll-driven 3D

The homepage section at `#working-together` shows three CSS perspective layers: people, shared understanding, and working software. Normal scrolling changes their rotation and separation alongside the FDE story. The viewport uses native scrolling throughout; there is no scroll capture, WebGL dependency, or continuous render loop.

On smaller or short screens, the stage is not sticky and each explanation remains in normal flow. Reduced motion uses static geometry and non-sticky text. Without JavaScript, the complete content and static geometry remain available.

## FDE responsibilities

The `/forward-deployed-engineering` page explains how a forward-deployed engineer works directly with the client:

1. Listen to the client and users.
2. Map the real process and exceptions.
3. Agree on a scoped outcome and acceptance criteria.
4. Prototype with the people who use it.
5. Build the software, AI workflow, or integration.
6. Review progress and choices directly with the client.
7. Test with representative data and business journeys.
8. Support adoption and an agreed improvement plan.

The illustrative client engagement follows a fictional service team's request-handling workflow. It is explicitly labelled fictional and reports no invented client outcomes.

## Industry coverage

The industries page contains construction/real estate, manufacturing, logistics, retail/e-commerce, professional services, healthcare operations, financial services, education, hospitality, energy/utilities, field service, and nonprofits. An open invitation covers businesses that do not fit one of those categories. Feasibility and domain requirements are scoped per engagement.
