# BundleBoB — The work and the numbers

A 60-second film. Twelve shots, five seconds each, in the edit order below. This is a production specification, not footage that exists. The site player uses the separately generated eight-second procedural film.

The story crosses an auto/equipment workshop, a fabrication floor, a field installation, and a back office. Use hired performers with permission, generic premises, and synthetic paperwork. No founder, employer, customer, personal identity, external logo, or real financial data appears. Frame hands and work rather than identifiable faces. Capture 4K, 24 fps, with room for a 16:9 master and 9:16 crop. Record clean room tone at every location.

Keep the visual language calm: natural materials, restrained green-gray and warm neutral colors, slow deliberate camera moves. The audience should recognize work being done, not a technology spectacle. The generated software UI is composited in post from supplied site assets so every letter and amount is correct.

## Voiceover with timecodes

Read warmly and matter-of-factly at roughly 130 words per minute. Leave space around the source values; let the visual carry those exact amounts.

- **00–05:** “The work keeps moving. But the numbers can take longer to catch up.”
- **05–10:** “In a workshop, on a production floor, or out in the field.”
- **10–15:** “Jobs finish. Parts arrive. Plans change.”
- **15–20:** “And the picture of what a job is making stays spread across systems.”
- **20–25:** “BundleBoB is built to bring that picture together.”
- **25–30:** “Connect the tools you already use. Agree on the job and the rules.”
- **30–35:** “Then compare the records, including the places where they disagree.”
- **35–40:** “Keep both values. Explain the difference. Give it a next step.”
- **40–45:** “So finance and operations start from the same understanding.”
- **45–50:** “With dedicated support to keep it accurate as your business changes.”
- **50–55:** “For businesses that run on jobs, and need to see them clearly.”
- **55–60:** “BundleBoB. The work and the numbers, in the same frame.”

## Ready-to-paste video prompts

Generate each physical shot as five seconds, 24 fps, 16:9. These are tool-neutral prompts, not a claim about any model's current API or capabilities. Apply the negative prompt to every physical shot: **No logos, brand marks, watermarks, legible personal data, identifiable faces, glamour lighting, futuristic overlays, distorted hands, extra fingers, duplicated tools, warped machinery, uncontrolled camera shake, or fast zooms.** Do not synthesize readable UI or exact financial values; composite the authored interface after generation.

1. **Workshop / 00–05.** Five-second cinematic documentary shot, waist-height wide composition inside a modest professional auto and equipment workshop. Vehicle on a lift at right, neatly organized parts cart at left, technician visible only from shoulders down. Slow lateral dolly left to right, soft morning daylight through high windows, realistic practical task lighting, muted green-gray and warm neutral palette. Show ordinary work progressing confidently. Reserve central space for a clean vertical crop. **Additional negatives:** no vehicle badge, no license plate, no dramatic sparks. **Prefer filming:** convincing workshop safety and tool use need review.
2. **Fabrication / 05–10.** Five-second medium close-up of gloved hands placing a finished machined metal part beside an unbranded physical drawing on a fabrication bench. Slow short push-in, shallow but usable depth of field, large soft side light with gentle practical highlights. Natural documentary realism; the part has stable geometry throughout. No readable dimensions required. **Additional negatives:** no changing part shape, no impossible calipers, no fake technical text. **Film if precision matters:** physical geometry and hand-tool contact are likely failure points.
3. **Field service / 10–15.** Five-second medium shot from behind a technician, frame cropped below the neck, closing a neutral equipment service panel and consulting an unbranded tablet. Static camera, overcast outdoor daylight, plausible industrial service setting with no company markings. Tablet screen is an even dark gray for later compositing. Deliberate, everyday motion. **Additional negatives:** no dangerous wiring, no readable screen, no disappearing hinge. **Prefer filming:** safe maintenance actions are easier to direct than generate.
4. **Back office / 15–20.** Five-second overhead close-up of three generic job sheets, a pencil, and an unbranded laptop on a clean but working office desk. Slow top-down slide, broad soft window light, restrained warm paper and dark green palette. Only abstract lines on the sheets; the laptop screen is blank neutral gray for a tracked composite. **Additional negatives:** no fake names, no gibberish headings, no luxury executive-office styling. Suitable for generation with screen replacement.
5. **Two sources / 20–25.** Five-second locked close-up of a matte unbranded office monitor seen square on, subtle desk edge at the bottom, neutral soft ambient light with no screen reflections. Screen is flat dark gray, perfectly stable, with clear corners for compositing two panels. Camera does not move. **Additional negatives:** no text, no spontaneous UI, no flicker, no warped screen. Best produced directly from the authored interface; no generated plate is required.
6. **Mapping / 25–30.** Motion-design direction for a video tool that accepts a supplied reference: retain the exact provided BundleBoB diagram, accounting and operations sources above a central mapping block, warm paper background and ink lines. Slow digital push, no more than four percent over five seconds. Preserve all typography and geometry. **Additional negatives:** no rewritten words, extra connectors, decorative particles, invented data, perspective tilt. **Use code rendering instead:** exact UI integrity is unlikely to survive generative transformation.
7. **Values diverge / 30–35.** Motion-design direction using the provided reconciliation reference: operations moves from 184,600 dollars to 196,450 dollars while accounting remains 184,600 dollars, then hold. Locked composition, clear source labels, synthetic-data disclosure remains visible. **Additional negatives:** no value changes other than the specified operation, no digits morphing into symbols, no floating dashboards. **Must use the procedural renderer:** exact values and timing are part of the meaning.
8. **Conflict / 35–40.** Motion-design direction using the supplied conflict-entry reference: reveal the 11,850 dollar difference and its explanation, approved addition not yet posted. Keep both source totals and the “no automatic write-back” line legible. Three-percent digital crop toward the entry, restrained warm accent. **Additional negatives:** no green “synced” state, no auto-correction, no hidden source, no invented text. **Must use code rendering and editorial compositing.**
9. **Shared review / 40–45.** Five-second medium overhead view of two people represented only by hands, reviewing a plain printed synthetic job report at a practical office table. One hand points, the other circles an action with a pencil. Soft daylight, slow lateral slide, natural documentary rhythm. The report layout stays stable and can be replaced in post. **Additional negatives:** no identifiable face, no handshake, no celebratory gestures, no duplicated pens. **Prefer filming:** coordinated hands and paper contact are prone to artifacts.
10. **Standing review / 45–50.** Five-second locked close-up of an unbranded weekly paper calendar beside a short review checklist on a desk. Soft meeting-room daylight, pencil at the edge of frame, a hand turns a single page slowly. Blank cells and abstract lines only; final labels added in post. **Additional negatives:** no dates or names synthesized, no paper melting, no multiple simultaneous page turns. Suitable for generation if the hand action is simplified.
11. **Work continues / 50–55.** Generate three separate clips for a five-second montage: an organized parts cart rolling left to right in a workshop; a finished metal part placed into protective packing on a fabrication bench; a service technician cropped below the neck closing an unbranded vehicle door. Match calm left-to-right movement, soft daylight, neutral documentary realism. Edit to 1.5, 1.5, and 2 seconds. **Additional negatives:** no brand marks, no changing objects, no flashy transitions. **Prefer filming** all three to maintain physical continuity.
12. **End card / 55–60.** Use the supplied authored BundleBoB end card unchanged: cream background, ink wordmark, warm accent, the line “The work and the numbers, in the same frame.” and bundlebob.com. Static five-second hold with a twelve-frame dissolve in. **Additional negatives:** no alternate spelling, animated letters, extra tagline, new logo, music visualization. **Render directly from code; do not generate the typography.**
