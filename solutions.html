How the engagement is intended to run

Five moves, in order

This is the intended sequence for an engagement, not a report from one that has happened. Each step is meant to make the next one cheap. We have not run it with a client yet.

Engagement sequence — illustrative
Map the cost structureStep 1
Sync two jobs in a sandboxStep 2
Pilot with one crewStep 3
Cut over in productionStep 4
Roll the rest of the portfolioStep 5

01 Map the cost structure

Time with your controller. We take your real cost codes, phases and cost types as they exist — you are not asked to reorganise your chart of accounts to fit a tool.

02 Sync two jobs in a sandbox

One straightforward job and one ugly one. The ugly one is the point: it surfaces vendor duplicates and retainage mismatches before they touch production.

03 Pilot with one crew

A single foreman, on their own phone, for a couple of weeks. If they will not use the screen, it gets fixed before anyone else sees it.

04 Cut over in production

End of week, with the old process still available afterwards. Nobody is asked to trust a sync they have not watched run.

05 Roll the rest of the portfolio

Every job after the first should be a clone of a working configuration. That is where the speed is meant to come from.

By trade

Which numbers come first

Mechanical

Spools and sheet metal

Fabrication releases and spool tracking through install, with productivity read against installed quantity rather than an eyeballed percent complete.

Electrical

Gear and lead times

Switchgear and transformer lead times against the schedule, and conduit and wire quantities that roll to job cost.

Plumbing

Underground to trim

Phase-level tracking from underground rough through trim-out, with inspection records attached to what triggered them.

Fire protection

Fab and inspection

Fabrication tickets tied to the release, and inspection sign-offs stored where the closeout package pulls from.

General contractors

Every trade, one view

Roll up trade-partner cost and schedule without asking each one to abandon the system they already run.

Service divisions

Work orders and T&M

Dispatch, T&M tickets and billing posting to the same job-cost structure as the construction side.

Prefab shops

Shop to site

Shop hours and released assemblies costed against the job they belong to, not a floating overhead bucket.

Finance teams

A WIP built from posted numbers

The reconciliation between systems done before the schedule is built, not after someone questions it.
